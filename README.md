# APR vs APY: How Compounding Changes Crypto Returns and What to Check Before Using OKX Earn

When comparing crypto staking, lending, or savings products, **APR and APY are not interchangeable**.

A product showing 10% APR and another showing 10% APY may produce different results over the same period. The gap comes from compounding, but compounding is only one part of the comparison. You also need to check whether the rate is fixed or variable, whether rewards are automatically reinvested, whether the advertised figure is estimated, and whether fees or redemption restrictions reduce the actual outcome.

## APR vs APY in simple terms

### What APR means

APR stands for **Annual Percentage Rate**.

In a basic savings or crypto rewards calculation, APR usually describes the annualized return **without including the effect of compounding**.

For example:

- Starting balance: $1,000
- APR: 10%
- One-year reward at a simple rate: $100
- Ending balance: $1,100

The calculation is:

text
Simple reward = principal × APR
$1,000 × 10% = $100


In crypto products, APR may be used for staking rewards, lending returns, liquidity incentives, or promotional rates. However, the exact meaning depends on the product terms. Some platforms use APR for a rate that assumes rewards are withdrawn rather than reinvested. Others may display an estimated annualized reward based on recent network or market conditions.

APR is also used in traditional lending. In that context, it can include certain borrowing costs and fees, so a loan APR is not always directly comparable with a crypto staking APR. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that loan APR can include the interest rate plus additional lender charges.

For crypto users, the important question is not only “What is the APR?” but also:

- Are rewards paid in the deposited asset or another token?
- Are rewards automatically added to the balance?
- Can you reinvest them manually?
- Is the advertised APR fixed or estimated?
- Does the rate change with market demand?
- Are there fees or delays when redeeming?

### What APY means

APY stands for **Annual Percentage Yield**.

APY includes the effect of compounding. If rewards are added to your balance and then begin generating additional rewards, the effective annual return can be higher than the equivalent non-compounded APR.

Assume:

- Starting balance: $1,000
- Nominal APR: 10%
- Monthly compounding
- No fees or rate changes

The APY is:

text
APY = (1 + APR ÷ compounding periods) ^ compounding periods − 1
APY = (1 + 0.10 ÷ 12) ^ 12 − 1
APY ≈ 10.47%


At the end of one year:

- Simple 10% APR: approximately $100 in rewards
- 10% APR compounded monthly: approximately $104.71 in rewards
- Difference: approximately $4.71 on a $1,000 balance

That difference is not magic money. It assumes the rate stays unchanged and the rewards are actually reinvested at the stated interval.

## APR and APY comparison

| Feature | APR | APY |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | Annual Percentage Rate | Annual Percentage Yield |
| Includes compounding? | Usually no | Yes |
| Common use | Staking rewards, lending rates, borrowing costs | Savings products and compounded earning products |
| Easier to compare? | Yes, when compounding rules are different or unclear | Yes, when the product compounds automatically |
| Main question | How much is the annualized base rate? | How much could the balance grow with compounding? |
| Main limitation | Can overstate or understate the practical result if compounding is ignored | Can look attractive when compounding is manual, conditional, or based on a changing rate |

The cleanest comparison is usually **APR against APR** or **APY against APY**. Comparing a 10% APR product with a 10% APY product without checking the terms can lead to the wrong conclusion.

## The formula that connects APR and APY

If the APR is nominal and compounds several times per year, the standard formula is:

text
APY = (1 + APR ÷ n)ⁿ − 1


Here, `n` is the number of compounding periods per year.

Examples using a nominal 10% APR:

| Compounding schedule | Approximate APY |
| --- | ---: |
| No compounding | 10.00% |
| Annual | 10.00% |
| Semiannual | 10.25% |
| Quarterly | 10.38% |
| Monthly | 10.47% |
| Daily | 10.52% |

The difference becomes larger when the rate is higher or the compounding interval is shorter. Still, the practical result depends on whether the product really compounds at that frequency.

A crypto platform may credit rewards daily, hourly, or at another interval without automatically reinvesting them. Receiving rewards more often does not always mean those rewards are being added to the earning balance. This is one reason the product documentation matters more than the large number shown beside the rate.

## Why crypto products make APR vs APY more complicated

With a traditional savings account, the interest rate and compounding rules are often relatively easy to understand. Crypto products can involve additional moving parts.

### The rate may change

OKX says that Earn offers can differ in APY, earnings schedule, redemption processing time, and source of earnings. The current Earn page also displays estimated APR figures for certain crypto assets, which means the shown rate can change rather than behaving like a guaranteed fixed deposit rate.

For Simple Earn, OKX documentation explains that the market APY reflects real-time lending and borrowing activity. If your selected minimum APY is higher than the current market APY, the order may not be matched and the unmatched assets may not generate earnings.

That creates a practical distinction:

- A fixed 10% APR for a defined term is easier to model.
- A variable 10% market APY is only a snapshot of current conditions.
- An estimated staking APR can change when network rewards, validator performance, or platform fees change.

### The displayed rate may be estimated

On-chain Earn involves staking through blockchain protocols. OKX states that estimated rewards are theoretical estimates rather than guaranteed returns, and actual rewards may be higher or lower. It also notes that costs, fees, and expenses related to operating the service may be deducted from rewards.

That means an advertised APR should be treated as a rate indicator, not a promise that your account will finish the year at exactly that percentage.

### The reward asset matters

A 10% return paid in a volatile token is not the same as a 10% return paid in dollars or a stablecoin.

Suppose you stake 1,000 units of a token and earn 10% APR. After one year, you may have roughly 1,100 units before considering compounding, fees, or changes in the reward rate. But if the token price falls by 20%, the dollar value of those holdings may still be lower.

The relevant questions are:

- Is the reward paid in the same asset?
- Is the asset a stablecoin, a major cryptocurrency, or a smaller token?
- Are rewards exposed to token price changes immediately?
- Are you comparing token-denominated returns or dollar-denominated returns?

APR and APY describe the quantity of assets earned. They do not remove market risk.

Dual Investment should not be evaluated using APR or APY alone. The product generally involves a target price and settlement date. Depending on market conditions at expiry, you may receive the original asset or another settlement asset.

The headline yield may be attractive, but your result depends on:

- The target price
- The expiry date
- The settlement currency
- The market price at settlement
- Whether you are comfortable receiving the alternative asset

This is not the same as placing funds in a simple interest account. A quoted annualized yield does not remove the possibility that the settlement outcome changes your asset exposure.

When comparing an on-chain APR with an exchange-based APY, check whether the numbers include:

- Validator or platform fees

- Unstaking delays
- Network inflation
- Reward distribution timing
- The possibility of receiving a liquid staking token instead of the original asset

The highest displayed percentage is not automatically the best choice if access to the funds is important.

## A practical example: comparing two crypto offers

Imagine two products:

- Product A: 8% APR with rewards paid monthly and no automatic reinvestment
- Product B: 7.7% APY with automatic compounding
- Starting balance: $1,000
- Rates remain unchanged for one year
- No fees, price changes, or taxes

Product A produces approximately:

text
$1,000 × 8% = $80


Ending balance: approximately $1,080.

Product B produces approximately:

text
$1,000 × 7.7% = $77


Ending balance: approximately $1,077.

In this example, Product A produces more because its nominal rate is higher, even though it does not compound.

Now change the terms:

- Product A: 7.5% APR, monthly compounding
- Product B: 7.7% APY

Product A’s effective APY is approximately:

text
(1 + 0.075 ÷ 12)^12 − 1 ≈ 7.76%


Now Product A is slightly ahead on paper. The difference is small, so other factors such as liquidity, asset risk, and rate stability may matter more than the headline number.

This is why the label alone is not enough. Convert both offers to the same basis, then compare the conditions behind the calculation.

## How to compare an APR or APY offer before subscribing

Use this checklist:

1. **Confirm whether the rate is APR or APY.**
   Do not assume a percentage includes compounding.

2. **Check whether compounding is automatic.**
   If rewards must be manually reinvested, the displayed APY may not be achievable without regular action.

3. **Check whether the rate is fixed or variable.**
   Market APY, estimated staking APR, and promotional rewards can change.

4. **Read the term and redemption rules.**
   Flexible access may have a lower rate. Fixed or locked products may offer a different return but reduce liquidity.

5. **Check limits and eligibility.**
   Promotional rates often apply only to certain assets, account types, balances, or regions.

6. **Identify the reward asset.**
   A token-denominated return can lose dollar value if the asset price falls.

7. **Look for fees.**
   Include trading fees, conversion spreads, platform fees, validator fees, and withdrawal costs where applicable.

8. **Review the risk source.**
   Lending, staking, liquidity provision, and structured products carry different risks.

9. **Compare the net result.**
   The best headline rate is not necessarily the best practical return after fees and restrictions.

10. **Check the live product page before subscribing.**
    Crypto rates can change faster than published articles.

## Should you choose APR or APY?

Neither metric is automatically better.

Choose based on what the product actually does:

- Use **APR** as a straightforward base-rate comparison when rewards are not compounded.
- Use **APY** when rewards are automatically reinvested and the compounding schedule is clear.
- Convert APR to APY when comparing two products that compound differently.
- Treat estimated or variable APY as a current indication, not a guaranteed annual outcome.
- Give liquidity and asset risk at least as much attention as the percentage.
